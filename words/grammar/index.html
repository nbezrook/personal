01 · START WITH THE PATTERN

Farsi فارسی

Go to Russian ↓

01 Put the verb near the end

A neutral Persian sentence typically puts the subject first and the verb last. With a direct object, a useful starting pattern is subject → object → verb. Time and place phrases often come before the verb.

من امروز به مدرسه می‌روم.

man emruz be madrese mi-ravam.

I go to school today.

The verb ending often identifies the subject, so a subject pronoun can be left out when the context is clear.

Reference: UT Austin · sentence structure.

02 Pronouns and “to be”

او covers “he” and “she.” Use شما for several people or to address one person politely.

A useful set of present forms

you · familiar singularتوtoهستی
he / sheاوuاست
weماmâهستیم
you · plural / politeشماshomâهستید

من خسته هستم. او خسته است.

man khaste hastam. u khaste ast.

I am tired. He / she is tired.

You will also meet short attached forms, such as خسته‌ام (khaste-am, “I am tired”). For “is,” است is the usual neutral form; هست can express existence or emphasis. The negative “I am not” is نیستم (nistam).

References: UT Austin · pronouns, short copulas, long copulas.

03 Nouns and counting

A common plural ending is ـها (-hâ). Other plurals occur, including -ân and borrowed Arabic forms, so learn unfamiliar plurals with the noun.

کتاب → کتاب‌ها

ketâb → ketâb-hâ

book → books

After an ordinary cardinal number, the noun normally stays singular: سه کتاب (se ketâb) means “three books.” یک کتاب (yek ketâb) can mean “one book” or “a book”; the suffix -i can also mark an indefinite noun, as in کتابی.

References: UT Austin · nouns, plural examples, indefinite nouns.

04 Ezafe connects a noun to its description

Link a noun to a following adjective or possessor with the sound -e, or -ye after certain vowels. This link is called ezafe. The adjective usually follows its noun.

کتابِ خوبِ من

ketâb-e khub-e man

my good book · literally “book–good–me”

خانهٔ من

khâne-ye man

my house

After a consonant, the linking vowel is usually heard without being written. The vowel marks in the book example make those links visible for learning.

Reference: UT Austin · ezafe.

05 Mark a specific direct object with را

را (râ) follows a definite or specific direct object. Put it after the whole noun phrase, including any adjectives or possessor.

من این کتاب را می‌خوانم.

man in ketâb râ mi-khânam.

I read this book.

Do not add it to the subject or automatically to every object. Its use depends on how specifically the object is identified in the sentence.

References: UT Austin · direct object marker part 1 and part 2.

06 Present tense: prefix, stem, ending

For many verbs, the present indicative has the pattern mi- + present stem + personal ending. Learn the present stem separately: it is often not predictable from the infinitive.

07 Simple past: keep the past stem

Remove the final ن (-an in the reading) from the infinitive to get the past stem. For رفتن (raftan), the stem is رفت (raft).

The third-person singular has no personal ending in this simple-past pattern. Add na- to negate it: نرفتم (na-raftam), “I did not go.”

References: UT Austin · past stem, negation.

08 Ask a question; learn a whole verb

A yes/no question can keep statement word order and use questioning intonation. In formal writing, آیا (âyâ) can introduce it.

آیا شما خسته هستید؟

âyâ shomâ khaste hastid?

Are you tired?

Useful question words include کجا (kojâ, where), چه (che, what), and چرا (cherâ, why).

Many verbs combine a noun or adjective with a verb. Learn the whole expression: کار کردن (kâr kardan) means “to work.” Conjugate its verb part: کار می‌کنم (kâr mi-konam), “I work”; کار کردم (kâr kardam), “I worked.”

References: UT Austin · questions, question words, compound verbs.

02 · WATCH THE ENDINGS

Russian Русский

Back to Farsi ↑

01 Begin with a simple sentence

Subject → verb → object is a useful neutral starting order. Endings mark grammatical roles, allowing word order to shift with emphasis and context.

Я читаю книгу.

I read a book / I am reading a book.

In ordinary present-tense identity statements, Russian usually leaves out “am / is / are”: Я студент. means “I am a student” (male speaker); Я студентка. uses the feminine noun. Russian has no direct equivalents of the English articles “a” and “the.”

Subject pronouns: я (I), ты (familiar singular you), он / она / оно (he / she / it), мы (we), вы (plural or polite you), они (they).

Reference: Cornell · sentence types, pronouns, and forms of address.

02 Noun gender shapes the adjective

Learn a noun with its gender. A consonant ending often signals masculine; -а / -я often signals feminine; -о / -е often signals neuter. These are clues, with exceptions: папа (“dad”) is masculine, and nouns ending in -ь can be masculine or feminine.

Новый · “new,” in the nominative
Gender / numberExampleMeaning
masculineновый домa new house
feminineновая книгаa new book
neuterновое окноa new window
pluralновые книгиnew books

Adjectives agree with nouns in gender, number, and case. These are nominative forms; other sentence roles may change both endings.

References: Cornell · gender, adjective agreement.

03 Six cases, six kinds of job

A case form helps show what a noun is doing in a sentence. Here is one noun, книга (“book”), in all six main cases. The uses shown are starting points; verbs and prepositions can require particular cases.

References: Cornell · case endings and case functions.

04 Present verbs change with the subject

Here are two common imperfective verbs. Their endings illustrate the two main conjugation patterns. Learn a verb’s actual forms rather than relying only on its infinitive ending.

Читать · read / говорить · speak

Я читаю. can mean either “I read” or “I am reading.” The context supplies the distinction that English often makes with a separate progressive form.

Reference: Cornell · conjugation classes.

05 Past tense agrees in gender and number

For many verbs, replace infinitive -ть with -л and select the appropriate ending. With читать, use читал (masculine), читала (feminine), читало (neuter), or читали (plural).

Она читала. Они читали.

She was reading. They were reading.

There are no separate first- and second-person past endings: “I” and “you” also select the form appropriate to the subject’s gender and number. Some verbs have irregular stems or omit the masculine -л, so learn their past forms individually.

Reference: Cornell · past tense.

06 Aspect changes the view of an action

Imperfective often presents an activity, repetition, or an action without foregrounding its endpoint. Perfective often presents a bounded event or its completion. Learn aspect partners together, such as читать / прочитать (“read”). Context matters; this is not simply a distinction between unfinished and finished past events.

Я буду читать книгу.

I will read / be reading a book. Imperfective future: a future form of быть + infinitive.

Я прочитаю книгу.

I will read the book through. Perfective future: the verb itself takes the personal ending.

Perfective verbs do not have an ordinary present-tense meaning: their non-past personal forms refer to the future. Both aspects can occur in the past.

Reference: Cornell · tense and aspect.

07 Have, have not, and questions

A common possession pattern is у + a genitive possessor + есть + the thing possessed. With нет (“there is no”), that thing takes the genitive too.

У меня есть книга. У меня нет книги.

I have a book. I do not have a book.

To negate an ordinary verb, usually put не before it: Я не читаю. means “I am not reading / I do not read.” Do not assume every negated direct object must be genitive; Russian also uses accusative objects under negation.

A simple yes/no question can keep the same word order and change its intonation: Ты читаешь? — “Are you reading?” Polite or plural: Вы читаете?

References: Cornell · possession and objects under negation, and questions.

03 · MATCH THE SUBJECT AND THE ENDING

French Français

Go to Chinese ↓

01 Learn a noun with its article

French nouns have grammatical gender: masculine or feminine. Articles match the noun’s gender and number. Learn un livre (“a book”) or une maison (“a house”) as a pair rather than learning the noun alone.

Common definite and indefinite articles
Noun“The”“A / some”
masculine singularle livreun livre
feminine singularla maisonune maison
pluralles livres / les maisonsdes livres / des maisons

Before a vowel sound or mute h, le and la become l’: l’amie, “the female friend.” Some words have an h aspiré that blocks this elision. A plural noun often adds -s, with exceptions; final plural -s is usually silent.

References: UT Austin · definite articles, indefinite articles, noun number.

02 Keep the subject with its verb

When a pronoun is the subject of an ordinary statement, keep it with the finite verb rather than routinely dropping it. Tu addresses one familiar person; vous addresses several people or one person politely. On takes a third-person singular verb and often means “we” in conversation.

Être · to be / avoir · to have

Nous sommes ici. J’ai un livre.

We are here. I have a book.

Je becomes j’ before a vowel sound, as in j’ai. Both verbs above are irregular: learn their forms directly.

References: UT Austin · subject pronouns, être, avoir.

03 Present tense: start with regular -er verbs

For a regular -er verb, remove -er and add the subject’s ending. Parler (“to speak”) has the stem parl-. Many common verbs follow other patterns or have spelling changes.

Parler · present indicative

Elle parle français.

She speaks French / she is speaking French.

The present can express a habit or an action happening now. The written endings -e, -es, -ent are often silent, so spelling alone does not tell you how different the forms sound.

References: UT Austin · regular -er verbs, present tense.

04 Adjectives agree with their noun

A regular adjective often adds -e for feminine and -s for plural: petit, petite, petits, petites. Learn irregular forms separately. An adjective already ending in -e often has the same masculine and feminine singular form.

Une petite maison. Des maisons blanches.

A small house. White houses.

Many adjectives follow the noun, but common ones such as petit often come before it. Placement can also change meaning, so learn an adjective in a short phrase.

Elles sont fatiguées.

They are tired. Here elles refers to a feminine plural group, so fatiguées has -es.

References: UT Austin · agreement and placement, adjectives before nouns.

05 Negate a sentence; ask a question

In standard written French, put ne … pas around the conjugated verb. Before a vowel sound, ne becomes n’. Conversational speech often drops ne.

Je ne parle pas français. Je n’ai pas de voiture.

I do not speak French. I do not have a car.

After a negated possession such as je n’ai pas, an indefinite article normally becomes de / d’. This is not a universal rule for all negatives: after être, for example, keep the indefinite article.

One useful yes/no pattern is est-ce que + statement. Intonation can also turn a statement into a question; inversion is another option.

Est-ce que vous parlez français ?

Do you speak French?

References: UT Austin · negation, articles under negation, yes/no questions.

06 Passé composé: an auxiliary and a participle

A common way to describe a past event is present avoir or être + past participle. Regular -er verbs form the participle in -é: parler → parlé. Many other participles are irregular.

J’ai parlé. Marie est arrivée.

I spoke / have spoken. Marie arrived.

Most verbs use avoir. A set of intransitive verbs, including arriver, uses être; pronominal verbs also use it. With non-pronominal être verbs, the participle agrees with the subject: arrivé, arrivée, arrivés, arrivées. Some verbs change auxiliary with their construction.

With avoir, the participle normally does not agree with the subject; a preceding direct object can trigger agreement. Pronominal verbs have additional agreement rules. Other past tenses, notably the imparfait, serve different purposes.

References: UT Austin · passé composé with avoir and être.

07 A useful future: aller + infinitive

Conjugate aller in the present, then leave the next verb in its infinitive. This futur proche is useful for intentions and anticipated events.

je vais · tu vas · il / elle / on va · nous allons · vous allez · ils / elles vont

Nous allons étudier. Je vais parler.

We are going to study. I am going to speak.

The second verb stays unconjugated: je vais parler, not je vais parle. French also has a separate simple future tense.

Reference: UT Austin · futur proche.

04 · FOLLOW THE ORDER AND THE PARTICLES

Chinese 中文

Try the quiz ↑

01 Mandarin begins with a familiar order

A useful neutral order is subject → verb → object. Verbs do not change form for “I,” “you,” or “they.” Context, time expressions, and aspect particles help express when and how an event occurs.

我喝茶。她喝茶。

Wǒ hē chá. Tā hē chá.

I drink tea. She drinks tea.

Time expressions often appear before the verb, either before or after the subject: 我今天学习中文。 (Wǒ jīntiān xuéxí Zhōngwén.), “I study Chinese today.” Word order also has topic-based variations; this is a starting pattern.

Reference: Palacký University · ChinGram grammar basics.

02 Pronouns: no verb conjugation to memorize

您 (nín) is a respectful singular “you.” The common third-person singular forms above differ in writing but are all pronounced tā. The plural suffix 们 (men) is common with personal pronouns; do not treat it as an ordinary plural ending to attach to every noun.

Reference: ChinGram · personal pronouns.

03 是 for identity; an adjective can be a predicate

Use 是 (shì) to link a subject with a noun identifying it. An adjective predicate normally does not need 是. A neutral positive adjective statement often uses 很 (hěn), whose force can be weaker than English “very.”

我是学生。她很忙。

Wǒ shì xuésheng. Tā hěn máng.

I am a student. She is busy.

For “have,” use 有 (yǒu): 我有书。 (Wǒ yǒu shū.), “I have books / a book.” For a person’s location, use 在 (zài): 我在家。 (Wǒ zài jiā.), “I am at home.” These patterns do different jobs from English “be.”

References: ChinGram · predicate types, 是, 有, and degree adverbs.

04 不 and 没 are different kinds of “not”

不 (bù) commonly negates habits, intentions, and states. 没 / 没有 (méi / méiyǒu) commonly denies that an event occurred, and also negates 有 (“have”). This is not simply a present-versus-past rule.

我不喝茶。我没有书。

Wǒ bù hē chá. Wǒ méiyǒu shū.

I do not drink tea. I do not have books / a book.

我昨天没去学校。

Wǒ zuótiān méi qù xuéxiào.

I did not go to school yesterday.

不是 is pronounced bú shì: bù changes to a rising tone before another fourth tone. Pinyin here uses ordinary lexical tone marks except where a change is explicitly shown.

References: ChinGram · 不 and 没 / 没有.

05 Count with a measure word

A usual counting pattern is number → measure word → noun. Learn the usual measure word with the noun. 个 (gè) is very common, but specific nouns often use a different one.

Three useful combinations
PhrasePinyinMeaning
三本书sān běn shūthree books
三个学生sān gè xuéshengthree students
一杯茶yī bēi cháa cup of tea

The noun itself does not add an English-style plural ending after a number. A demonstrative can use the same pattern: 这本书 (zhè běn shū), “this book.”

Reference: ChinGram · noun measure words.

06 Put the possessor before 的

A useful possession pattern is possessor + 的 + noun. The particle 的 (de) also connects many other modifiers to a following noun.

我的书。老师的书。

Wǒ de shū. Lǎoshī de shū.

My book. The teacher’s book.

Modifiers come before the noun. 的 is not compulsory in every modifier phrase: certain short adjectives and close personal relationships can omit it. Start with the possession pattern, then learn other combinations in context.

Reference: ChinGram · structural particle 的.

07 Question particles and question words

Add 吗 (ma) to a statement to ask a straightforward yes/no question. You do not need to invert the subject and verb.

你喝茶吗？

Nǐ hē chá ma?

Do you drink tea?

A question word normally occupies the place of the information you are asking about. With 什么 (shénme, “what”), a basic question is:

你喝什么？

Nǐ hē shénme?

What do you drink?

Do not automatically add 吗 to this question-word pattern.

References: ChinGram · 吗 and interrogative pronouns.

08 了 marks aspect or a change, not a universal past tense

After a verb, 了 (le) can present an event as realized or completed. A time phrase can establish when it happened. Verbs do not receive a different past-tense conjugation.

我昨天买了三本书。

Wǒ zuótiān mǎi le sān běn shū.

I bought three books yesterday.

A sentence-final 了 can mark a new situation or change: 我不喝茶了。 (Wǒ bù hē chá le.), “I no longer drink tea.” These functions can overlap. Not every past sentence needs 了, and 了 does not always describe the past.

References: ChinGram · aspectual 了 and sentence-final 了.
