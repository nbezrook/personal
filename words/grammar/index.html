01 · START WITH THE PATTERN

Farsi فارسی

Go to Russian ↓

Beginner grammar

Intermediate ↓Advanced ↓

01 Put the verb near the end

A neutral Persian sentence typically puts the subject first and the verb last. With a direct object, a useful starting pattern is subject → object → verb. Time and place phrases often come before the verb.

من امروز به مدرسه می‌روم.

man emruz be madrese mi-ravam.

I go to school today.

The verb ending often identifies the subject, so a subject pronoun can be left out when the context is clear.

Reference: UT Austin · sentence structure.

02 Pronouns and “to be”

او covers “he” and “she.” Use شما for several people or to address one person politely.

03 Nouns and counting

A common plural ending is ـها (-hâ). Other plurals occur, including -ân and borrowed Arabic forms, so learn unfamiliar plurals with the noun.

کتاب → کتاب‌ها

ketâb → ketâb-hâ

book → books

After an ordinary cardinal number, the noun normally stays singular: سه کتاب (se ketâb) means “three books.” یک کتاب (yek ketâb) can mean “one book” or “a book”; the suffix -i can also mark an indefinite noun, as in کتابی.

References: UT Austin · nouns, plural examples, indefinite nouns.

04 Ezafe connects a noun to its description

Link a noun to a following adjective or possessor with the sound -e, or -ye after certain vowels. This link is called ezafe. The adjective usually follows its noun.

کتابِ خوبِ من

ketâb-e khub-e man

my good book · literally “book–good–me”

خانهٔ من

khâne-ye man

my house

After a consonant, the linking vowel is usually heard without being written. The vowel marks in the book example make those links visible for learning.

Reference: UT Austin · ezafe.

05 Mark a specific direct object with را

را (râ) follows a definite or specific direct object. Put it after the whole noun phrase, including any adjectives or possessor.

من این کتاب را می‌خوانم.

man in ketâb râ mi-khânam.

I read this book.

Do not add it to the subject or automatically to every object. Its use depends on how specifically the object is identified in the sentence.

References: UT Austin · direct object marker part 1 and part 2.

For many verbs, the present indicative has the pattern mi- + present stem + personal ending. Learn the present stem separately: it is often not predictable from the infinitive.

07 Simple past: keep the past stem

Remove the final ن (-an in the reading) from the infinitive to get the past stem. For رفتن (raftan), the stem is رفت (raft).

08 Ask a question; learn a whole verb

A yes/no question can keep statement word order and use questioning intonation. In formal writing, آیا (âyâ) can introduce it.

آیا شما خسته هستید؟

âyâ shomâ khaste hastid?

Are you tired?

Useful question words include کجا (kojâ, where), چه (che, what), and چرا (cherâ, why).

Many verbs combine a noun or adjective with a verb. Learn the whole expression: کار کردن (kâr kardan) means “to work.” Conjugate its verb part: کار می‌کنم (kâr mi-konam), “I work”; کار کردم (kâr kardam), “I worked.”

References: UT Austin · questions, question words, compound verbs.

01 Connect a past event to the present

The present perfect combines a past participle + present copula. For a regular participle, add -e to the past stem: khând → khânde. The personal part changes: khânde-am, khânde-i, khânde ast.

من این کتاب را خوانده‌ام.

man in ketâb râ khânde-am.

I have read this book.

Negation attaches to the participle: na-khânde-am, “I have not read.” This tense can also report information learned indirectly; its uses do not match English present perfect in every context.

Reference: UT Austin · Persian Online.

02 Use the subjunctive after wants and obligations

Many present subjunctives use be- + present stem + personal ending, without mi-. Compare mi-ravam (“I go”) with be-ravam (“that I go”). Expressions such as bâyad (“must / should”) commonly take this form.

باید فردا بروم.

bâyad fardâ be-ravam.

I must go tomorrow.

The negative uses na- instead of be-: na-ravam. Learn exceptions separately: “to be” uses bâsham, and compound verbs do not all follow one prefix pattern.

Reference: UT Austin · Persian Online.

03 Add information with a که clause

A clause introduced by که (ke) can follow a noun phrase and tell the reader more about it. Keep the verb inside that clause in its own normal position; then continue the surrounding sentence.

خواهرم، که در تهران زندگی می‌کند، معلم است.

khâharam, ke dar Tehrân zendegi mi-konad, moallem ast.

My sister, who lives in Tehran, is a teacher.

Here the clause adds extra information about an already identified person. The same linking word also appears in other kinds of subordinate clauses; do not translate every ke as “who.”

Reference: UT Austin · Persian Online.

03 · EXPLORE MORE STRUCTUREAdvanced grammarPlace one past event before another · Describe an unreal situation with اگر · Express uncertainty about something already done

Work with more complex sentences and distinctions in meaning. Select the heading again to collapse this section.

01 Place one past event before another

The past perfect uses past participle + past form of “to be”: khânde budam (“I had read”), khânde budi, khânde bud. It locates an event before another past reference point.

وقتی رسیدم، او رفته بود.

vaqti rasidam, u rafte bud.

When I arrived, he / she had left.

The arrival is simple past; the earlier departure is past perfect. Negate the participle: na-rafte bud, “had not left.” A word meaning “already” is not required.

Reference: UT Austin · Persian Online.

02 Describe an unreal situation with اگر

In a common hypothetical pattern, اگر (agar, “if”) introduces a condition, and past-form verbs with mi- can express imagined circumstances and results. These forms need not describe an actual past event.

اگر بیشتر درس می‌خواندم، بهتر می‌فهمیدم.

agar bishtar dars mi-khândam, behtar mi-fahmidam.

If I studied more, I would understand better.

Context distinguishes a present hypothetical reading from a past counterfactual one. Do not assign an English tense from the Persian verb form alone; first identify the time and whether the condition is real or imagined.

Reference: UT Austin · Persian Online.

03 Express uncertainty about something already done

The perfect subjunctive combines past participle + subjunctive of “to be”: rafte bâsham, rafte bâshi, rafte bâshad. It can place a completed event under a modal expression rather than assert it directly.

شاید او رفته باشد.

shâyad u rafte bâshad.

Perhaps he / she has left.

Compare rafte ast (“has left,” stated as a fact) with shâyad rafte bâshad (“may have left”). Negation goes on the participle: na-rafte bâshad. The surrounding expression determines whether the meaning is possibility, necessity, or another modal reading.

Reference: UT Austin · Persian Online.

01 Begin with a simple sentence

Subject → verb → object is a useful neutral starting order. Endings mark grammatical roles, allowing word order to shift with emphasis and context.

Я читаю книгу.

I read a book / I am reading a book.

In ordinary present-tense identity statements, Russian usually leaves out “am / is / are”: Я студент. means “I am a student” (male speaker); Я студентка. uses the feminine noun. Russian has no direct equivalents of the English articles “a” and “the.”

Subject pronouns: я (I), ты (familiar singular you), он / она / оно (he / she / it), мы (we), вы (plural or polite you), они (they).

Reference: Cornell · sentence types, pronouns, and forms of address.

02 Noun gender shapes the adjective

Learn a noun with its gender. A consonant ending often signals masculine; -а / -я often signals feminine; -о / -е often signals neuter. These are clues, with exceptions: папа (“dad”) is masculine, and nouns ending in -ь can be masculine or feminine.

Here are two common imperfective verbs. Their endings illustrate the two main conjugation patterns. Learn a verb’s actual forms rather than relying only on its infinitive ending.

Я читаю. can mean either “I read” or “I am reading.” The context supplies the distinction that English often makes with a separate progressive form.

Reference: Cornell · conjugation classes.

05 Past tense agrees in gender and number

For many verbs, replace infinitive -ть with -л and select the appropriate ending. With читать, use читал (masculine), читала (feminine), читало (neuter), or читали (plural).

Она читала. Они читали.

She was reading. They were reading.

There are no separate first- and second-person past endings: “I” and “you” also select the form appropriate to the subject’s gender and number. Some verbs have irregular stems or omit the masculine -л, so learn their past forms individually.

Reference: Cornell · past tense.

06 Aspect changes the view of an action

Imperfective often presents an activity, repetition, or an action without foregrounding its endpoint. Perfective often presents a bounded event or its completion. Learn aspect partners together, such as читать / прочитать (“read”). Context matters; this is not simply a distinction between unfinished and finished past events.

Я буду читать книгу.

I will read / be reading a book. Imperfective future: a future form of быть + infinitive.

Я прочитаю книгу.

I will read the book through. Perfective future: the verb itself takes the personal ending.

Perfective verbs do not have an ordinary present-tense meaning: their non-past personal forms refer to the future. Both aspects can occur in the past.

Reference: Cornell · tense and aspect.

07 Have, have not, and questions

A common possession pattern is у + a genitive possessor + есть + the thing possessed. With нет (“there is no”), that thing takes the genitive too.

У меня есть книга. У меня нет книги.

I have a book. I do not have a book.

To negate an ordinary verb, usually put не before it: Я не читаю. means “I am not reading / I do not read.” Do not assume every negated direct object must be genitive; Russian also uses accusative objects under negation.

A simple yes/no question can keep the same word order and change its intonation: Ты читаешь? — “Are you reading?” Polite or plural: Вы читаете?

References: Cornell · possession and objects under negation, and questions.

01 Choose a direction of motion

For unprefixed motion verbs, distinguish one direction from repeated, return, or otherwise non-one-way motion. On foot, the basic pair is идти / ходить; by transport, it is ехать / ездить. Both members are imperfective.

Сейчас я иду в школу. Каждый день я хожу в школу.

I am walking to school now. I go to school every day.

This contrast is not the same as perfective versus imperfective aspect. Adding a prefix can change aspect and introduce a more specific meaning, so learn a prefixed motion verb as a whole.

Reference: Cornell · Russian Grammar.

02 Compare with чем or a genitive

A short comparative can be followed by чем (“than”). In suitable noun comparisons, a genitive phrase can express the second term without чем. Keep the intended comparison clear.

Анна выше, чем Иван. Анна выше Ивана.

Anna is taller than Ivan. Anna is taller than Ivan.

Use a comma before чем. The genitive alternative can be ambiguous in more complex sentences; a full чем clause helps show exactly what you are comparing.

Reference: Cornell · Russian Grammar.

03 Match который to its noun and its clause

The relative word который (“who / which / that”) takes its gender and number from the noun it describes. Its case comes from its job inside the relative clause, not from the noun’s case in the main clause.

Это книга, которую я читаю.

This is the book that I am reading.

Книгу would be the direct object of читаю, so the relative pronoun is feminine accusative которую. Relative clauses are separated from the main clause by commas.

Reference: Cornell · Russian Grammar.

01 Compress a relative clause into a participle

A present active participle describes a noun through an action in progress. It comes from an imperfective verb and takes adjective endings. For many verbs, replacing the final -т in the third-person plural form with -щий gives the masculine nominative form: читают → читающий.

Студент, читающий книгу, сидит у окна.

The student reading a book is sitting by the window.

The participle agrees with its noun in gender, number, and case. Such phrases are especially common in written language. A conversational alternative here is студент, который читает книгу.

Reference: Cornell · Russian Grammar.

02 Sequence actions with an adverbial participle

A perfective adverbial participle can package a completed action before the main action. Many vowel-final past stems take -в: прочитать → прочитав, “having read.” The form does not change for gender or number.

Прочитав письмо, Анна позвонила брату.

Having read the letter, Anna called her brother.

In this standard pattern, the same person performs both actions: Anna reads and calls. Avoid a dangling phrase with a different doer. These constructions are most typical of written style; learn irregular forms separately.

Reference: Cornell · Russian Grammar.

03 Imagine a different situation with бы

Unreal conditions commonly use если бы + past-form verb, with бы and a past-form verb in the result clause too. Despite their shape, these verbs can refer to an imagined present or future as well as an unreal past.

Если бы у меня было время, я бы прочитал эту книгу.

If I had time, I would read this book. (male speaker)

The speaker uses masculine прочитал; a female speaker would use прочитала. Context supplies the time reference. The unstressed particle бы cannot stand first in its clause.

Reference: Cornell · Russian Grammar.

01 Learn a noun with its article

French nouns have grammatical gender: masculine or feminine. Articles match the noun’s gender and number. Learn un livre (“a book”) or une maison (“a house”) as a pair rather than learning the noun alone.

03 Present tense: start with regular -er verbs

For a regular -er verb, remove -er and add the subject’s ending. Parler (“to speak”) has the stem parl-. Many common verbs follow other patterns or have spelling changes.

04 Adjectives agree with their noun

A regular adjective often adds -e for feminine and -s for plural: petit, petite, petits, petites. Learn irregular forms separately. An adjective already ending in -e often has the same masculine and feminine singular form.

Une petite maison. Des maisons blanches.

A small house. White houses.

Many adjectives follow the noun, but common ones such as petit often come before it. Placement can also change meaning, so learn an adjective in a short phrase.

Elles sont fatiguées.

They are tired. Here elles refers to a feminine plural group, so fatiguées has -es.

References: UT Austin · agreement and placement, adjectives before nouns.

05 Negate a sentence; ask a question

In standard written French, put ne … pas around the conjugated verb. Before a vowel sound, ne becomes n’. Conversational speech often drops ne.

Je ne parle pas français. Je n’ai pas de voiture.

I do not speak French. I do not have a car.

After a negated possession such as je n’ai pas, an indefinite article normally becomes de / d’. This is not a universal rule for all negatives: after être, for example, keep the indefinite article.

One useful yes/no pattern is est-ce que + statement. Intonation can also turn a statement into a question; inversion is another option.

Est-ce que vous parlez français ?

Do you speak French?

References: UT Austin · negation, articles under negation, yes/no questions.

06 Passé composé: an auxiliary and a participle

A common way to describe a past event is present avoir or être + past participle. Regular -er verbs form the participle in -é: parler → parlé. Many other participles are irregular.

J’ai parlé. Marie est arrivée.

I spoke / have spoken. Marie arrived.

Most verbs use avoir. A set of intransitive verbs, including arriver, uses être; pronominal verbs also use it. With non-pronominal être verbs, the participle agrees with the subject: arrivé, arrivée, arrivés, arrivées. Some verbs change auxiliary with their construction.

With avoir, the participle normally does not agree with the subject; a preceding direct object can trigger agreement. Pronominal verbs have additional agreement rules. Other past tenses, notably the imparfait, serve different purposes.

References: UT Austin · passé composé with avoir and être.

07 A useful future: aller + infinitive

Conjugate aller in the present, then leave the next verb in its infinitive. This futur proche is useful for intentions and anticipated events.

je vais · tu vas · il / elle / on va · nous allons · vous allez · ils / elles vont

Nous allons étudier. Je vais parler.

We are going to study. I am going to speak.

The second verb stays unconjugated: je vais parler, not je vais parle. French also has a separate simple future tense.

Reference: UT Austin · futur proche.

01 Set the scene with the imparfait

Use the imparfait for a past situation, habit, or action viewed as ongoing. Use the passé composé to present an event as a bounded whole. A narrative often combines them to distinguish background from what happened next.

Je lisais quand le téléphone a sonné.

I was reading when the telephone rang.

Lisais supplies the ongoing background; a sonné presents the event that interrupts it. The choice is about how the speaker presents the event, not simply whether it lasted a long or short time.

Reference: UT Austin · Tex’s French Grammar.

02 Place direct and indirect object pronouns

In an ordinary declarative sentence, object pronouns normally precede their verb: le / la / les replace direct objects; lui / leur can replace an indirect object referring to a person with verbs such as donner à. With these pairs, le / la / les comes first.

Je donne le livre à Marie. Je le lui donne.

I give the book to Marie. I give it to her.

In a compound tense, put the pronouns before the auxiliary: Je le lui ai donné. Pronouns attached to an infinitive generally precede that infinitive. Affirmative commands follow a different placement pattern.

Reference: UT Austin · Tex’s French Grammar.

03 Join sentences with qui and que

Choose a relative pronoun by its role inside the new clause: qui is the subject; que is the direct object. Either can refer to a person or a thing. Before a vowel sound, que becomes qu’.

Le livre qui est sur la table est le livre que je lis.

The book that is on the table is the book that I am reading.

In the first clause, the book is what “is on the table.” In the second, “I” is the subject and the book is what I read. Do not add another object pronoun for the same noun.

Reference: UT Austin · Tex’s French Grammar.

01 Distinguish a required action from an assertion

The subjunctive appears after expressions such as il faut que (“it is necessary that”). Learn the trigger together with the dependent verb form. It is a mood selected by the construction, not a special future tense.

Il faut que tu finisses ce travail.

You must finish this work.

Tu finisses is subjunctive; the indicative would be tu finis. Common verbs have irregular forms, such as soit and ait. For an obligation with an unspecified doer, an infinitive is possible: Il faut travailler.

Reference: UT Austin · Tex’s French Grammar.

02 Pair the tenses in a si clause

For an imagined present or future condition, use si + imparfait → conditional. For an unreal past condition, a common pattern is si + plus-que-parfait → past conditional. The two clauses have different jobs and different tense forms.

Si j’avais le temps, je lirais ce livre.

If I had time, I would read this book.

Compare the unreal past: Si j’avais eu le temps, j’aurais lu ce livre. (“If I had had time, I would have read this book.”) In these conditional patterns, do not put the conditional immediately after si.

Reference: UT Austin · Tex’s French Grammar.

03 Go back to an earlier point in the past

The plus-que-parfait combines the imparfait of avoir or être with the past participle. Choose the auxiliary as you would for the passé composé. It presents an event before another past reference point.

Quand je suis arrivé, elle était déjà partie.

When I arrived, she had already left. (male speaker)

The departure happened before the arrival. With être, the participle agrees with its subject: elle était partie, ils étaient partis. With avoir, agreement depends on a preceding direct object; do not automatically match the subject.

Reference: UT Austin · Tex’s French Grammar.

01 Mandarin begins with a familiar order

A useful neutral order is subject → verb → object. Verbs do not change form for “I,” “you,” or “they.” Context, time expressions, and aspect particles help express when and how an event occurs.

我喝茶。她喝茶。

Wǒ hē chá. Tā hē chá.

I drink tea. She drinks tea.

Time expressions often appear before the verb, either before or after the subject: 我今天学习中文。 (Wǒ jīntiān xuéxí Zhōngwén.), “I study Chinese today.” Word order also has topic-based variations; this is a starting pattern.

Reference: Palacký University · ChinGram grammar basics.

02 Pronouns: no verb conjugation to memorize

Common personal pronouns

您 (nín) is a respectful singular “you.” The common third-person singular forms above differ in writing but are all pronounced tā. The plural suffix 们 (men) is common with personal pronouns; do not treat it as an ordinary plural ending to attach to every noun.

Reference: ChinGram · personal pronouns.

03 是 for identity; an adjective can be a predicate

Use 是 (shì) to link a subject with a noun identifying it. An adjective predicate normally does not need 是. A neutral positive adjective statement often uses 很 (hěn), whose force can be weaker than English “very.”

我是学生。她很忙。

Wǒ shì xuésheng. Tā hěn máng.

I am a student. She is busy.

For “have,” use 有 (yǒu): 我有书。 (Wǒ yǒu shū.), “I have books / a book.” For a person’s location, use 在 (zài): 我在家。 (Wǒ zài jiā.), “I am at home.” These patterns do different jobs from English “be.”

References: ChinGram · predicate types, 是, 有, and degree adverbs.

04 不 and 没 are different kinds of “not”

不 (bù) commonly negates habits, intentions, and states. 没 / 没有 (méi / méiyǒu) commonly denies that an event occurred, and also negates 有 (“have”). This is not simply a present-versus-past rule.

我不喝茶。我没有书。

Wǒ bù hē chá. Wǒ méiyǒu shū.

I do not drink tea. I do not have books / a book.

我昨天没去学校。

Wǒ zuótiān méi qù xuéxiào.

I did not go to school yesterday.

不是 is pronounced bú shì: bù changes to a rising tone before another fourth tone. Pinyin here uses ordinary lexical tone marks except where a change is explicitly shown.

References: ChinGram · 不 and 没 / 没有.

05 Count with a measure word

A usual counting pattern is number → measure word → noun. Learn the usual measure word with the noun. 个 (gè) is very common, but specific nouns often use a different one.

Three useful combinations
PhrasePinyinMeaning
三本书sān běn shūthree books
三个学生sān gè xuéshengthree students
一杯茶yī bēi cháa cup of tea

The noun itself does not add an English-style plural ending after a number. A demonstrative can use the same pattern: 这本书 (zhè běn shū), “this book.”

Reference: ChinGram · noun measure words.

06 Put the possessor before 的

A useful possession pattern is possessor + 的 + noun. The particle 的 (de) also connects many other modifiers to a following noun.

我的书。老师的书。

Wǒ de shū. Lǎoshī de shū.

My book. The teacher’s book.

Modifiers come before the noun. 的 is not compulsory in every modifier phrase: certain short adjectives and close personal relationships can omit it. Start with the possession pattern, then learn other combinations in context.

Reference: ChinGram · structural particle 的.

07 Question particles and question words

Add 吗 (ma) to a statement to ask a straightforward yes/no question. You do not need to invert the subject and verb.

你喝茶吗？

Nǐ hē chá ma?

Do you drink tea?

A question word normally occupies the place of the information you are asking about. With 什么 (shénme, “what”), a basic question is:

你喝什么？

Nǐ hē shénme?

What do you drink?

Do not automatically add 吗 to this question-word pattern.

References: ChinGram · 吗 and interrogative pronouns.

08 了 marks aspect or a change, not a universal past tense

After a verb, 了 (le) can present an event as realized or completed. A time phrase can establish when it happened. Verbs do not receive a different past-tense conjugation.

我昨天买了三本书。

Wǒ zuótiān mǎi le sān běn shū.

I bought three books yesterday.

A sentence-final 了 can mark a new situation or change: 我不喝茶了。 (Wǒ bù hē chá le.), “I no longer drink tea.” These functions can overlap. Not every past sentence needs 了, and 了 does not always describe the past.

References: ChinGram · aspectual 了 and sentence-final 了.

02 · BUILD ON THE BASICSIntermediate grammarTalk about experience with 过 · Show the result of an action · Make a comparison with 比

Connect ideas and develop the patterns introduced above. Select the heading again to collapse this section.

01 Talk about experience with 过

Put the aspect particle 过 (guo) after a verb to say that someone has had an experience. The focus is on whether it has happened before, rather than on narrating one completed event.

我去过北京。

Wǒ qù guo Běijīng.

I have been to Beijing.

Negate the experience with 没（有）, keeping 过: 我没去过北京。 (Wǒ méi qù guo Běijīng.), “I have never been to Beijing.” Do not treat 过 as a general past-tense ending.

Reference: Palacký University · ChinGram.

02 Show the result of an action

A resultative complement follows a verb and says what the action achieves. Compare 看 (kàn, read / look) with 看完 (kàn wán, finish reading), or 听 (tīng, listen) with 听懂 (tīng dǒng, understand by listening).

我看完了这本书。

Wǒ kàn wán le zhè běn shū.

I finished reading this book.

The complement precedes 了. To say the result has not been reached, use 没 before the verb and omit this aspectual 了: 我没看完。 means “I did not finish reading.” It does not say that no reading took place.

Reference: Palacký University · ChinGram.

03 Make a comparison with 比

Use A + 比 (bǐ) + B + adjective to say A has more of a quality than B. The comparison itself supplies the contrast; do not insert the ordinary predicate marker 很 before the adjective in this pattern.

今天比昨天冷。

Jīntiān bǐ zuótiān lěng.

Today is colder than yesterday.

The first term is what you are describing; the second is the comparison point. Both can be time expressions, as here. For “not as … as,” learn the separate 没有 comparison pattern rather than translating English word by word.

Reference: Palacký University · ChinGram.

03 · EXPLORE MORE STRUCTUREAdvanced grammarPut an affected object before the verb with 把 · Describe what happened to something with 被 · Focus on how or when an event happened

Work with more complex sentences and distinctions in meaning. Select the heading again to collapse this section.

01 Put an affected object before the verb with 把

A useful pattern is subject + 把 (bǎ) + identified object + verb + result or other completion. It tells what someone does to an object, often emphasizing its changed state or location.

我把这本书看完了。

Wǒ bǎ zhè běn shū kàn wán le.

I finished reading this book.

The object is identified, and 完 supplies the result. A bare verb alone is generally insufficient in this use. Negation and modal verbs precede 把: 我没把这本书看完。 means “I did not finish reading this book.”

Reference: Oxford · Chinese grammar database.

02 Describe what happened to something with 被

A passive pattern is affected subject + 被 (bèi) + doer + verb phrase. It puts the entity undergoing an action first. The doer can be omitted when unknown or unimportant.

我的自行车被偷了。

Wǒ de zìxíngchē bèi tōu le.

My bicycle was stolen.

Here the bicycle is affected and the thief is unnamed. 被 often appears with an unwelcome result, but it is not restricted to bad events. Chinese does not use a 被 construction for every situation expressed by an English passive.

Reference: Oxford · Chinese grammar database.

03 Focus on how or when an event happened

For an event already treated as having happened, 是 … 的 (shì … de) can highlight a detail such as its time, place, or manner. Put the focused detail after 是 in this pattern.

我是坐火车来的。

Wǒ shì zuò huǒchē lái de.

I came by train. (It was by train that I came.)

The point is the means of travel, not simply that the arrival occurred. Compare a plain event report such as 我来了。 The 是 … 的 focus construction is not a universal past-tense form.

Reference: Palacký University · ChinGram.
